AV-2008 alarm with Shelly: the project

Arming, disarming and notifications from the phone for an old AV-GAD EasyLoader AV-2008 alarm panel, using a Shelly Plus Uni Wi-Fi module.

By Shmilovitz · October 2026. Planned and built with the help of Claude.

Works in the browser only, with no AI service. Press / to search.

Why this project

More than once the alarm was armed and went off, the system called me, and I had no way to disarm it from where I was. This panel model (AV-2008, not the AV-2008D) cannot be disarmed during that phone call. Now it disarms from the app, from anywhere.

The final push came from a message in the WhatsApp group of the neighbourhood where I live, after alarms went off on a Saturday morning (translated from Hebrew; the street name is removed):

A neighbour, in the neighbourhood WhatsApp group

Good morning, what's going on with the alarms first thing on Saturday morning on […] Street???

Whoever runs a home alarm, please make sure you can monitor it and stop it remotely when you're not home. Isn't that the whole idea?

The panel now arms and disarms from the Shelly app, and the phone gets a notification when the alarm goes off. The module connects to three existing points on the panel (wiring plan): the key-switch input, the "armed" output and the "alarm" output. The panel itself was not modified. What it can't do is listed in section 9. Please read the disclaimer before using any of it.

1. The idea

The panel is an AV-GAD EasyLoader AV-2008 from 2005, with no Wi-Fi and no app. A Shelly Plus Uni connects to points the panel already has (where they are: section 5):

WhatHow
Arm / disarmShelly output 1 gives a 1-second pulse between KEY and -V, like an external key switch. Each pulse toggles the state.
"Armed" stateInput 1 reads the panel's ON output, which switches to minus while the panel is armed.
"Alarm!"Input 2 reads the A1 output, which switches to minus during an alarm.
PowerThe Shelly runs from the panel's 12V, including from its battery during a power cut.

Why the Plus Uni: it has 2 isolated outputs, 2 inputs and Wi-Fi, and runs on 12V with no extra supply. Because KEY toggles, you cannot tell what a tap did without the status input.

3. Wiring plan

5 cores connect 8 Shelly wires to 5 panel terminals; the sixth core is a spare. The colours were chosen so a likely mistake does no harm: the green core is the spare, because the Shelly's green wire goes to -V.

Cable coreShelly wiresPanel terminal
RedRed (VAC1) + white (ANALOG IN)+12V, screw 2
BlackBlack (VAC2) + green (GND) + one OUT1 wire-V, screw 3
WhiteThe other OUT1 wireKEY, top screw of the left block
YellowOrange (IN1)ON, screw 16
BrownBrown (IN2)A1, screw 15
GreenNot connectedNot connected

Unused Shelly wires are each insulated on their own: yellow, blue, grey (+5V), purple, and both OUT2 wires. The antenna (a thin black wire with a sleeve on its tip) is left untouched. It doesn't matter which OUT1 wire goes where: OUT1 is an isolated switch that works both ways.

4. Soldering

The cable was soldered to the Shelly leads at a desk, and the finished unit was installed afterwards.

  1. Identify the wires. There are 10 coloured leads, stripped and tinned at the factory, plus 4 black output wires. The order matches the drawing on the box.
  2. Check green against black. It measured about 500 Ω. That is normal: there is a rectifier diode between them, so the test is done in diode mode.
  3. Each joint: slide the heat-shrink on first, then twist, solder and shrink. The -V joint, with 4 wires, needs a wider sleeve.
  4. Unused wires: each one gets its own cap.
  5. Into the box: the Shelly goes in the box, the cable leaves through a drilled hole in the bottom, and the lid closes.

A note on the photos: these joints are far from a professional finish, but they hold, they are fully insulated, and everything works.

5. Finding the panel terminals

Screw 1 on the bottom strip is Earth (⏚), not +12V. The first guide assumed the strip started with +12V, and the meter showed no voltage between screws 1 and 2.

How it was found:

  1. The wiring diagram in AV-GAD's English manual (v2.17, page 62) shows an Earth terminal for the cold-water pipe before +12V.
  2. The printed Hebrew manual (v2.12, page 11) shows an earth line from the left-most screw, and says the earth must go to the earth screw.
  3. The board has a green-yellow wire on the left of the strip.
  4. Between screws 2 and 3 the meter read about 12V, with plus on screw 2.
Screw1234–11121314151617
Terminal⏚ Earth+12V-Vzones 1–8-VYEORA1ONSLO

KEY is the top screw of the vertical block on the left (marked KEY STR on the board).

6. Installing in the cabinet

The connections were made with the 220V on and the battery disconnected, in two stages. The alarm's own breaker could not be found, and at 12V it is enough to work carefully with an insulated screwdriver, away from the transformer's 220V block.

  1. The box went outside the steel cabinet, next to the electrical board, and the cable went inside.
  2. Stage A, power only: red to screw 2, black to screw 3. The Shelly's LED came on.
  3. Shelly setup (next section), while KEY, ON and A1 were still disconnected.
  4. OUT1 check: meter in continuity mode between the white core and screw 3. A tap in the app gave a 1-second beep.
  5. Stage B: white to KEY, yellow to screw 16 (ON), brown to screw 15 (A1).
  6. The battery went back on, and the panel stayed disarmed.

7. Shelly setup

The settings only stuck once they were set directly from a computer on the same network. The first Save in the app didn't take, and both channels stayed in Toggle mode. The Shelly was added in the Shelly Smart Control app over Bluetooth, on the 2.4GHz Wi-Fi.

Why Detached is critical

In Toggle mode, the ON input would switch OUT1, and the panel would arm and disarm by itself.

Where these settings are in the app (version 3.77)

  • Detached and power-on default: device page, gear icon (Device settings), Input/output settings.
  • Auto off: the plain clock icon (Timer). The clock icon with a + is the pulse counter; leave it alone.
  • Armed state on the button: the double-gear icon (Application settings), Input state display.
  • After saving, reopen the page and check that the choice is still selected.

8. Phone notifications

Notifications don't come by themselves. Each one is a Scene in the app, which runs in the Shelly cloud. The app shows the Shelly as two cards: the first is channel 1, the arm/disarm button; the second, marked "(2)", is channel 2, whose output is not connected.

SceneWhenDoStatus
AlarmInput State 2, Turn on, TriggerNotify action: "Alarm triggered!"Working
ArmedInput State 1, Turn on, TriggerNotify action: "Alarm armed"Not set up yet
DisarmedInput State 1, Turn off, TriggerNotify action: "Alarm disarmed"Not set up yet

Tested: the Armed input switches on about a second after the arming tap and off after the disarming tap. On the first arming, the Alarm input also read on for about 2 seconds, so watch for a false "alarm" notification when arming.

9. What this solution can't do

The Shelly acts like one key switch and two status lights. Everything else stays on the keypad, which talks to the panel over a closed data bus (YE / OR).

  • You can't see which zone triggered the alarm, only that it went off.
  • You can't change the panel's settings or programming (codes, delays, zones), the way the keypad can.
  • You can't arm with specific zones bypassed, or part-arm, as the keypad can (for example 075).
  • You can't see before arming whether a zone is open. The panel simply doesn't arm, and you only notice because no "armed" notification arrives.
  • There are no separate arm and disarm commands: every tap toggles.
  • No alerts for tamper, faults, low battery or the phone line. A power-cut alert is possible through the voltmeter input, which is wired but not set up yet; it also needs the home router to stay powered.
  • No panel event log, only the Shelly's activity log (when the inputs changed).
  • No user code: anyone with access to the Shelly account can disarm.
  • Remote control and notifications need Wi-Fi, internet and the Shelly cloud.

For full control from the phone you need a newer panel with its own app, for example RISCO LightSYS+ or PIMA Force.

11. Tips

  • Daily use: tap once, then wait for the notification. If nothing arrives within a minute, a door or window is probably open. Don't tap again "to make sure": a second tap disarms.
  • Security: the Shelly account can now disarm the house. Use a strong password and two-factor authentication on the account, and set a password on the Shelly's local web interface.
  • If the internet is down, remote control and notifications stop, but the alarm itself keeps working normally.
  • Firmware: after any update, check that Detached, Turn OFF and Auto off are still set (section 7).
Safety

The transformer in the alarm cabinet carries 220V AC. Don't open it or touch its terminal block. This page describes one specific installation; check your own panel's labels and manual before connecting anything.
